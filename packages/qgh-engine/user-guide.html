QGH Simulator

Training use only. Verify all operational procedures against current authorised publications and local instructions.

STARTING AN EXERCISE

  1. Choose Single Aircraft QGH or Tactical QGH. The optional first-run Guided Familiarisation can be skipped or reopened from GUIDED TOUR.
  2. Enter runway orientation, outbound track, inbound / final track, aircraft profile, range, speed and turn rate.
  3. For a single aircraft, enter its callsign. You may include or omit that callsign in single-aircraft voice calls. Tactical calls must identify the aircraft.
  4. Select Normal QGH or U/S Compass, then start the exercise.
  5. Use D/F transmit, turn, speed, clock and advance controls. Termination opens the recorded flight-path review.

LOCAL VOICE CONTROL

PRESS TO TALK BY DEFAULT

Hold PTT, say one complete controller call, then release. The guided familiarisation teaches these live exercise calls first; setup and review remain deliberately manual in the tour. Open VOICE and prepare local voice before first browser use. Continuous Listening is optional and has a clear stop action. Recognition remains local only; there is no cloud speech fallback.

TACTICAL QGH

Say the complete callsign first: “Raven Twenty One turn right heading zero six zero”. A unique designator such as Raven is also accepted; use the full callsign if two aircraft share it.

A callsign may also be a number from 100 to 999. For callsign 387, say TREE EIGHT SEVEN before the instruction.

“Raven Twenty One continue zero six zero” while Raven Twenty One is already turning that way

“Raven Twenty One transmit for D/F”

“Raven Twenty One turn right now” · “Raven Twenty One stop turn now”

“Raven Twenty One stop following leader”

Use aviation digits for headings: zero, one, two, tree, four, fife, six, seven, eight, niner. Plain phrase variations are accepted when the aircraft, action and required heading are clear. Give one clear action at a time: conditional, negated, wrong-unit and chained calls are rejected without changing the exercise. “Terminate exercise” opens the safety confirmation; say “Confirm termination” or “Keep exercise.”

PILOT REPLIES, PRIORITY AND MUTE

Flight commands take effect as soon as they are accepted. The aircraft keeps moving and turning while the pilot speaks; a reply never pauses flight or delays the next accepted instruction. Red text above the homing display shows the accepted action or report, and the affected control is highlighted.

  1. Interrupt a reply: hold PTT to stop the pilot audio, give your new instruction, then release. Manual exercise controls remain usable during speech and recognition failure.
  2. Replace a turn: “Turn right 060” followed by “Turn left heading 010” immediately changes the commanded turn direction and target. The new pilot reply is Roger, turning left, zero one zero, [callsign]. The aircraft follows the configured turn rate; its heading does not jump to 010.
  3. Default — no headphones: pilot replies are muted. Red feedback, aircraft D/F and continuous voice control still work. No manoeuvre depends on hearing the pilot.
  4. Headphone readbacks: connect and wear headphones, open VOICE, then select HEADPHONES · PILOT READBACKS. Read the warning, play the headphone test, confirm that you heard it through your headphones, and choose ENABLE PILOT REPLIES. The test temporarily disables recognition so its words cannot issue commands. Aircraft continue flying.
  5. Four bundled male voices: the web app includes an offline pilot-voice pack in its initial download. Single aircraft uses Michael; tactical aircraft A–D use Michael, Fenrir, Puck and George respectively. Voices remain assigned to their aircraft when you change selection. Unfamiliar callsigns may be spelled. No device voice or online speech service is substituted.
  6. Pilot speech pace: the same packaged voices and target of approximately 100 words per minute apply on laptops and phones. Short phrases and pauses can vary. Replies combine pre-generated audio clips locally; there is no per-command neural inference or cloud request. This affects headphone audio only, not aircraft motion, controller recognition or muted transmission timing. Commands execute first without waiting for audio.
  7. Controller priority: with Continuous Listening on, recognition remains active during headphone pilot speech. Detected controller speech cancels the old reply; an accepted new command executes immediately and receives a fresh reply. The old reply does not resume. PTT takes priority immediately. Give a complete new call; an interruption alone does not change flight.
  8. Mute at any time: open VOICE and choose MUTE PILOT REPLIES or switch the readback option off, before removing headphones. Muting stops sound without cancelling a manoeuvre, red feedback or simulated aircraft D/F. Pilot replies start muted on every page/session and when the page is hidden.
  9. Device changes: a reported audio-device change mutes pilot replies and requires a fresh headphone test. Browsers cannot reliably prove that headphones are connected or receiving the sound; some route changes are not reported. Your confirmation is not automatic detection. Speaker audio may enter the microphone and execute unintended commands, so never enable readbacks on speakers.
  10. Offline preparation: keep the app open online until the initial offline download completes. Speech recognition retains its separate SET UP OFFLINE VOICE step. If a download, memory or audio error occurs, pilot replies stay silent; manual controls, red acknowledgements and aircraft D/F remain available. Browser storage can be cleared by you or the device, requiring another download.

Aircraft D/F updates during its simulated transmission. At release, the last indication is held for two seconds, then clears. A new transmission replaces the held indication. Ground indication is off. Muted replies still generate timed aircraft transmissions and red acknowledgements.

Normal heading reports use the measured heading, not the assigned target. U/S Compass replies acknowledge turning or stopping without revealing a heading. Tactical replies retain the addressed callsign even if you select another aircraft before playback.

Orbits — both procedures: use Orbit left, Orbit right, Left hand orbit or Right hand orbit, or the ORBIT buttons. An orbit starts from the aircraft’s current position and heading, at the selected speed and turn rate. The pilot reports each completed 360° circle and continues circling, with red feedback and D/F during the report. No heading is read out in U/S Compass.

Continue or Continue orbit keeps the existing orbit and cancels a pending resume instruction. Resume normal (also a button) finishes the current circle, then flies straight on the actual heading at which the orbit began. At the exact start of a circle, it can resume immediately. A new turn-to-heading call—including Continue zero six zero in Normal QGH—overrides the orbit immediately at the selected rate. In U/S Compass, Stop turn now rolls out on the current heading instead. Tactical calls need the aircraft callsign; a separate orbit command to a follower releases that aircraft from formation. A leader’s orbit retains its attached followers.

An unchanged speed and turn rate produce a closed circular path. Changing speed during the orbit changes its radius; the simulator does not teleport the aircraft back to the old position. Completion is detected at the next quarter-second flight sample; busy-channel reports wait until the channel is available.

Report a heading when passing: in Normal QGH, say Report heading passing three two five, Report passing heading three two five or Report passing three two five. Any heading 000–360 is accepted (360 means 000). This arms a one-time report; it does not change the aircraft’s turn or heading. The pilot reports Heading passing three two five when the aircraft crosses that heading, with red feedback and aircraft D/F. In Tactical QGH, put the aircraft’s callsign first. Each aircraft has one pending report; a newer valid request replaces it. Changing aircraft selection, stopping the microphone or giving another turn does not cancel the report. Restarting or terminating the exercise clears it. Heading reports remain unavailable with a U/S compass.

Reports wait while the radio is busy. If the crossing occurred more than two simulated seconds earlier—including during Advance flight 1 min—the pilot says Heading passed instead of Heading passing. D/F always shows that aircraft’s position during its actual transmission, then holds the last reading for two seconds. Muted pilot audio still gives the red report and D/F.

INFORMATIONAL RT AND LIMITS

Recognised informational calls such as “Surface wind two three zero degrees ten knots”, “Runway in use two three”, and “Overhead turn will be right longer way for outbound track zero six five” receive ROGER and aircraft D/F, without changing flight controls. ROGER means receipt, not execution.

The QGH / DF RT reference preserves the supplied phraseology for study. It is not a list of fully implemented commands. Descent, landing, visual reports, aircraft system checks and speechless PTT-click procedures are not simulated by this radio update. Recognised unsupported requests display NOT SIMULATED rather than claiming compliance. Unclear messages and unknown callsigns do not produce a guessed manoeuvre.

Give one clear call at a time. A conditional, correction, negation, unsupported unit or chained instruction is rejected as a whole; it does not execute an earlier-looking fragment. Repeat the intended instruction as a new clear call. Do not combine weather, conditional instructions and turns in one call.

OFFLINE AUDIO

Web v4.4.3 includes its four-voice pilot pack in the initial offline download. Controller recognition has a separate initial voice-setup step. Pilot replies are composed from packaged audio clips, with no device-voice or remote fallback. If audio is unavailable, captions and timed aircraft D/F continue without sound. Windows and Android packages are rebuilt only after this web candidate is verified. Verify recognition and replies in airplane mode on the actual device before training.

TACTICAL QGH

02–04 AIRCRAFT

Configure aircraft callsigns, profiles, levels, speed, turn rate and range with the visible setup controls. Levels default in 1,000 ft separation. For formation flight, select formation, leader and members before starting.

On phones, aircraft tabs use a compact two-column grid so up to four callsigns and their leader/following status are visible together. Tap a callsign to select it, or its colour-coded TX button to request D/F. Full selected-aircraft details remain in the controls.

CALLSIGN DISCIPLINE

Use the exact callsign visible in the left rail as the first words of every tactical call. A single unique designator is accepted, but the full callsign remains the clearest RT practice.

NOTES

  • Normal QGH uses assigned-heading turns. U/S Compass uses only left now, right now and stop turn now.
  • D/F is live during aircraft transmission, then frozen for a two-second hold. QDM is homing and QTE is true bearing; neither is the aircraft heading.
  • Speed and rate of turn affect manoeuvrability and turn radius. Tactical formation wingmen retain leader speed until separated.
  • The review records the continuous flight path, reference radials, commands and replay cursor. Use the replay-speed control to review efficiently.

PRIVACY · NATIVE APPS

Effective 5 September 2026. QGH Simulator is provided by Flt Lt Balaram Reddy for training, not operational air traffic control.

Microphone access is optional and used only for local controller-command recognition after you enable PTT or Continuous Listening. The app does not upload or save microphone recordings. Recognised commands and exercise tracks are held for the current exercise and review, not sent to a server. Manual controls work without microphone permission.

The native apps contain no advertising, analytics or account system. The Android app does not request Internet permission. Recognition models are stored locally. Optional pilot speech passes generated reply text to your device's installed speech service, selecting only a voice reported as not requiring a network connection. The device's speech service is provided separately by its vendor; QGH does not select a cloud voice.

Windows may retain local preferences and model caches; Android retains the extracted model but not exercise history. Restart an exercise to replace its in-memory data. Close the app to clear that session. Android's App info → Storage → Clear storage removes its local app data. Windows app data is under %APPDATA%\qgh-simulator; remove it only with the app closed if you want to erase its preferences and caches. Revoke microphone access in your device's privacy settings at any time.

For privacy questions, open the project support page in your browser: github.com/reds-aviation/qgh-voice/issues. Do not post personal information, audio recordings or sensitive training information publicly. Browser/PWA hosting is separate: its hosting provider receives ordinary web requests when you access or update the website.
